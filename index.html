Research

Interst : Dark Matter & Neutrino

My work connects theoretical model building with phenomenological constraints, numerical analysis, and modern search methods. In addition to these, I'm interested in testing models by using future experiments(HK, FCC, DUNE, and so on). Publication numbers correspond to the list below.

Dark Matter

WIMP Dark Matter from a $Z_4$ Flavor Symmetry

I investigate whether flavor structure can simultaneously explain dark-matter stability and suppress its scattering with ordinary matter. In a $Z_4$ lepton-flavor model, dark matter couples to a flavored mediator associated with the muon and tau sectors.

The symmetry protects dark-matter stability, while tree-level scattering with nucleons and electrons is absent. Loop-induced interactions nevertheless make the model testable in future direct-detection experiments.

[1] Phys. Rev. D 106, 035017 (2022) ↗
Neutrinos

Neutrino Physics in Gauged $U(1)_{L_\mu-L_\tau}$ Models

I study gauged $U(1)_{L_\mu-L_\tau}$ models as frameworks for neutrino masses, oscillations, and the muon $g-2$ anomaly. The symmetry produces a predictive flavor structure in the neutrino mass matrix.

We evaluated constraints from $Z$–$Z'$ mixing, meson decays, atomic parity violation, scalar-sector unitarity, and the $T$ parameter. An extended scalar sector can restore phenomenological viability.

[3] JHEP 12 (2024) 018 ↗
Machine Learning

Machine-Learning Searches for Flavor Models

I apply reinforcement learning to discrete searches in theories of flavor. In Froggatt–Nielsen models, the algorithm learns charge assignments capable of reproducing quark and lepton masses and mixing angles.

Extending the method to flaxion models produced more than 150 realistic quark-sector solutions connecting flavor physics with the strong-CP problem.

[2,4] JHEP 12 (2023) 021; JHEP 10 (2025) 043 ↓
